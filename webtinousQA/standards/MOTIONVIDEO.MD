## 1. Scope and production choices

Establish the audience, purpose, actual deliverables, demonstrated workflow, language, duration range, aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, audio/caption needs, editable-source requirements, distribution, and budget. Existing approved production choices continue without renewed approval.

For a new production project, ask before selecting unresolved substantial tooling or providers. Remotion, React, TypeScript, Three.js, React Three Fiber, Playwright, FFmpeg, ElevenLabs, and cloud rendering are possible tools; none is universally required. Use an existing suitable workflow where available. Do not assume that every request needs a launch film, tutorial, 3D, narration, music, UHD output, or a new production application.

A recommendation may propose a launch film and one tutorial, with fast review exports before final masters. Confirm the actual scope instead of silently expanding it. Reuse approved budget and account constraints; do not change subscriptions or purchase services without the necessary authorization.

## 2. Product truth and source evidence

**Inspect the supplied product and verify what it actually does.** Do not invent features, UI controls, integrations, AI capabilities, supported platforms, prices, dates, testimonials, customer logos, benchmarks, or security assurances.

Source code and documentation establish different facts from a successful runtime demonstration. A roadmap entry, mock, unfinished route, or hidden feature is not proof of a shipped capability. Match substantive final claims to reviewed evidence and the actual captured product/version.

For a repository-based production, retain source identity and relevant changes. For other products, record the supplied build or other available identity. A boolean “verified” field or a checksum records a review or integrity check, not semantic truth by itself.

If a claim or workflow cannot be verified, omit it, label it accurately, or identify that specific production step as incomplete. Never fabricate a successful screen to hide unavailable functionality.

## 3. Story and design

**Write a shot list or storyboard before polishing the animation.** Record shot purpose, duration, copy, supporting claim, real capture, framing, action/result, narration, captions, and relevant sound cues.

A launch piece should establish context, show the product, support its claims with real evidence, and offer a truthful next action. A tutorial should show prerequisites, starting state, ordered actions, visible outcomes, completion check, and any useful verified recovery route.

Keep the design coherent, readable, and consistent with the product's accepted brand. Use motion and depth only when they help the story. Keep UI demonstrations large enough to understand at the actual delivery size. Maintain state continuity across edits or clearly signal a reset or separate example.

## 4. Authentic capture and privacy

**Use the real browser, native app, device, terminal, or other actual product surface.** Set a reproducible starting state and a suitable recording environment. Use authorized sample data and isolate private credentials and sessions from public assets and archives.

Inspect recordings for readable labels, full action/result coverage, lead/tail, continuity, unrelated notifications, and sensitive content. Align cursor, click markers, zooms, and callouts to actual interactions and element bounds. Do not add fictitious clicks, missing prerequisites, fake persistence, or a success state that did not occur.

Match source capture quality to final needs. Upscaling does not recover missing detail; disclose material limitations. Preserve original recordings or approved source references as required by the project.

## 5. Reusable source and deterministic rendering

Where production is code-driven, separate product configuration, source media, claims, timeline, captions, and audio from reusable scene logic. Use validated, versioned inputs and local reviewed assets where appropriate. A second product should primarily change configuration and capture-specific work, not require rewriting the shared renderer.

For frame-based rendering, derive visible animation from the requested frame/time and immutable inputs. Avoid wall-clock state, unseeded randomness, accumulated transforms, or asynchronous asset races that make an isolated frame unreliable. Check representative repeated and out-of-order renders in the tested environment; do not promise identical pixels across all hardware.

For timeline/editor workflows, preserve editable sources, linked assets, version identity, and repeatable export settings. Do not impose a code renderer on a project whose accepted editor already meets the need.

## 6. Audio, captions, and providers

**Use the accepted provider, supplied media, authorized voices, and licensed assets.** Ask before introducing a new substantial or paid service. Do not silently substitute a different voice or provider when a specific one was agreed.

Review the actual script before generation. Cache successful paid outputs where appropriate, retain sanitized receipts, and investigate ambiguous paid timeouts before retrying. Never expose API keys or private account state in source bundles, public media, or deliverables.

Build captions from actual speech and reliable alignment. If timing is unavailable, use an appropriate verified alignment or review workflow; do not invent timestamps. Keep phrases readable and clear of essential UI. Retiming or editing the script is preferable to cutting words or rushing narration to fit an arbitrary slot.

Mix for the agreed delivery destination. Review intelligibility, pronunciation, synchronization, music/SFX balance, clipping, fades, and the encoded final mix. Loudness, channel layout, sample rate, and peak targets should follow the actual delivery specification rather than a universal preset.

Missing credentials or audio tools may allow silent drafts and completed independent work. Label them accurately; a placeholder voice, tone, or sample clip is not the agreed finished narration.

## 7. Branding, assets, and credits

**Keep product identity, studio attribution, and third-party rights distinct.** Webtinous · by Continous Group attribution is required for productions made under these standards. Keep it separate from the demonstrated product identity, and preserve accurate third-party notices.

Keep an appropriately sized asset record with provenance, license/usage notes, local path, review status, and measured properties where useful. Record content hashes for important evidence or reproducibility needs. Exclude private raw footage, secrets, caches, and unnecessary customer data from shareable packages.

## 8. Quality assurance and delivery

**Render representative scenes and a short encoded draft early.** Review complete final exports for claims, capture continuity, text/caption legibility, timing, cropping, callout alignment, audio, credits, and clean endings. Check actual dimensions, frame rate, duration, codecs, audio streams, and decode behavior using available media tools.

A successful still or short UHD sample proves only that sample. Do not label a draft, partial render, or upscaled low-resolution capture as an unqualified full-quality master. Record resource or access limits and the exact remaining step.

Deliver the agreed finished files, editable source, approved assets, captions where requested, actual setup/export instructions, evidence/QA notes, and limitations. Do not publish externally merely because production is complete. The task is complete only when the agreed outputs actually exist and pass their applicable checks.

## Practical examples and reference

### Delivery specification template

| Item | Agree or inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Outputs | Launch, tutorial, short clip, animation, or another actual request. |
| Audience and story | Intended viewer, verified claims, and required workflow/outcome. |
| Picture | Aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, color/output format, and readability. |
| Sound and text | Language, authorized voice, captions, music/SFX, and destination audio targets. |
| Tooling and budget | Existing renderer/editor, accepted providers, accounts, and limits. |
| Handoff | Final files, editable sources, assets/rights, evidence, and repeatable exports. |

An approved template can fill routine creative defaults; it must not silently commit the user to an unresolved paid provider, new stack, or unsupported output claim.

### Scene and evidence record

For each important shot, record purpose, exact claim/copy, source/runtime evidence, capture asset, timing, annotations, narration, and caption references. Review the claim's actual wording against its evidence.

Capture a complete tutorial action/result chain with consistent state. A cut may shorten waiting, but it must not conceal a required setup step or fabricate continuity. Verify actual encoded exports rather than relying only on the editor preview.

### Completion gate

An HD film plus a successful UHD sample is a valid partial UHD result when labeled precisely. It is not a finished UHD master. Missing provider credentials may permit scripts, capture, reusable tooling, and silent drafts; the agreed narrated final remains incomplete until the actual audio and final checks exist.

## Acceptance checklist

- **Agreement:** The outputs, major tooling, providers, credits, and budget are established.
- **Truth:** Product claims and demonstrated outcomes have corresponding evidence.
- **Craft:** Readable footage, clean timing, accurate captions, and final audio were reviewed.
- **Export:** Actual complete files pass relevant encoding and playback checks.
- **Delivery:** Editable sources, rights, instructions, and limitations accompany the agreed final outputs.
